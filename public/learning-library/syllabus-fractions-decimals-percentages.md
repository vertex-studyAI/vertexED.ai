## Both tiers: fractions and terminating decimals

A proper positive fraction has numerator smaller than denominator. An improper fraction has numerator at least as large as denominator; a mixed number separates its whole and fractional parts. For instance 11/4=2 3/4=2.75. Simplify a fraction by dividing top and bottom by their highest common factor. A terminating decimal is an exact fraction with a power-of-ten denominator: 0.045=45/1000=9/200.

To convert a fraction to a decimal, divide numerator by denominator, keeping place values in the quotient. A fraction in lowest terms terminates in base ten exactly when the denominator contains no prime factors except 2 and 5. Thus 7/40 terminates because 40=2³×5, while 1/6 repeats because its denominator still contains 3. This test explains behaviour; it does not replace carrying out a requested conversion.

## Both tiers: percentages and operators

Percent means per hundred. Divide a percentage by 100 to get a decimal multiplier, and multiply a decimal by 100 to express it as a percentage. Thus 0.08=8%=2/25, while 1.35=135%=27/20. A percentage can exceed 100%; it then represents more than one whole. Use the same reference whole when comparing forms.

To find 3/5 of 70, divide 70 into five equal parts then take three, or multiply 70×3/5=42. To find 12% of 250, multiply 250×0.12=30. A fraction of one quantity is not automatically the same fraction of another: specifying the whole is essential. If 18 of 24 students walk, the fraction is 18/24=3/4 and the percentage is 75%. Ratios compare parts: a red:blue ratio 3:5 means red is 3/8 of the combined total, not 3/5.

## Higher / Extended: recurring notation and long division

A recurring decimal has a block of digits that repeats forever. In these notes 0.(27) means 0.272727… and 0.1(6) means 0.166666… . The usual overbar or dots on the first and last recurring digits represent the same thing. The non-repeating prefix is outside the recurring block. Finite display digits are not an exact recurring representation: 0.333333 on a calculator is not the exact value 1/3.

Long division reveals the cycle because a repeated remainder leads to the same future digits. In 5÷6, 50÷6 leaves remainder 2 after the first decimal digit 8; 20÷6 produces 3 and leaves 2 again. Therefore 5/6=0.8(3). To convert a recurring decimal back, use algebra to align identical infinite tails, subtract and solve for the original number.

## Higher / Extended: aligning recurring tails

If x=0.(36), then 100x=36.(36). Subtract x to cancel the repeating tail: 99x=36, hence x=36/99=4/11. If there is a non-repeating prefix, align both the start and the period. For x=0.2(7), use 100x=27.(7) and 10x=2.(7), giving 90x=25 and x=5/18.

The subtraction must remove every recurring digit, not merely the visible first few. For x=1.2(34), 1000x=1234.(34) and 10x=12.(34), so 990x=1222. This gives 611/495, including the integer part. Substitute back by division or estimate its size. Cambridge Core does not require recurring notation or these conversions; Core learners should finish the first two sections and the Core-labelled practice.

## Worked example 1

Express 3 7/8 as an improper fraction, decimal and percentage.

1. Multiply the whole part by 8 and add 7: 24+7=31.

2. Divide 31 by 8 to obtain 3.875; multiply by 100 to express as a percentage.

Answer: 31/8, 3.875 and 387.5%.

## Worked example 2

[Higher / Extended] Convert 0.4(18) to an exact fraction.

1. Let x=0.4181818… . Then 1000x=418.1818… and 10x=4.1818… .

2. Subtract to get 990x=414, hence x=414/990=23/55.

Answer: 23/55.

## Misconceptions

- 0.6% equals 0.6 as a multiplier.
  Correction: 0.6%=0.006. Divide the percentage number by 100.

- A ratio 2:7 gives a fraction 2/7 of the total.
  Correction: There are nine total parts, so the first share is 2/9.

## Flashcards

### How do you turn a percentage into a multiplier?
Divide by 100.

### What is 0.375 as a fraction?
375/1000=3/8.

### How do you convert a mixed number to an improper fraction?
Multiply the whole part by the denominator and add the numerator.

### Which reduced denominators terminate in base ten?
Those with only 2 and 5 as prime factors.

### How do you eliminate a recurring tail?
Multiply to align identical tails, subtract, then solve.

## Practice 1 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Express 2 7/20 as an improper fraction, decimal and percentage.

Answer: 47/20, 2.35 and 235%.

1. 2×20+7=47.

2. Divide by 20 and then multiply the decimal by 100 for the percentage.

## Practice 2 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Convert 0.35% to a decimal and a simplest fraction.

Answer: 0.0035 and 7/2000.

1. Divide 0.35 by 100.

2. Write 35/10000 and divide top and bottom by 5.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Give 0.0035.

## Practice 3 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Calculate 7/8 of 96 and 125% of 64.

Answer: 84 and 80.

1. 96÷8×7=84.

2. 125%=1.25=5/4, so 64×5/4=80.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Use the fraction as an operator to get 84.

- Handle a percentage exceeding 100 to get 80.

## Practice 4 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] In a group, 21 of 28 learners cycle. Express this as a fraction and percentage. The cycle:non-cycle ratio is 3:1; explain why the fraction is not 3/1.

Answer: 3/4 and 75%; the ratio contains four total parts.

1. Reduce 21/28 by 7.

2. A part-to-part ratio uses a different denominator from a part-to-whole fraction.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Give 3/4 and 75%.

- Explain the four-part whole.

## Practice 5 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Write 0.072 as a simplest fraction and convert 13/40 to a decimal.

Answer: 9/125 and 0.325.

1. 72/1000 reduces by 8 to 9/125.

2. Multiply 13/40 by 25/25 to obtain 325/1000.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Simplify the first fraction.

- Give the exact terminating decimal.

## Practice 6 — extension · 2 marks

[GCSE Higher; IGCSE Extended] Convert 0.(54) to a simplest fraction, showing the subtraction.

Answer: 6/11.

1. Let x=0.(54); 100x−x=54.

2. 99x=54, so x=54/99=6/11.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Align and subtract the repeating tails.

- Reduce to 6/11.

## Practice 7 — extension · 2 marks

[GCSE Higher; IGCSE Extended] Convert 0.3(12) to a simplest fraction. State which digits repeat.

Answer: 103/330; the block 12 repeats.

1. 1000x=312.(12) and 10x=3.(12).

2. 990x=309, so x=309/990=103/330.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Identify the non-recurring prefix and repeating block.

- Form the correct difference and fraction.

## Practice 8 — extension · 2 marks

[GCSE Higher; IGCSE Extended] Use division to express 7/12 as a recurring decimal. Explain why writing 0.5833 is not exact.

Answer: 0.58(3); 0.5833 is only a finite approximation.

1. 70÷12 gives 5 remainder 10; 100÷12 gives 8 remainder 4.

2. 40÷12 gives 3 remainder 4, so the 3 repeats indefinitely.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Show the repeated remainder and notation.

- Distinguish finite approximation from exact value.

## Revision guide

Equivalent forms describe the same value but may make different operations easier. A fraction of a total differs from a part-to-part ratio. Recurring conversion uses exact algebra, not rounding displayed digits.
