## The regular -er pattern

For a regular French -er verb such as parler, remove -er and add the present-tense ending for the subject. The forms are je parle, tu parles, il/elle/on parle, nous parlons, vous parlez and ils/elles parlent. Several written endings may sound alike, so spelling cannot always be recovered from sound alone. A subject pronoun is normally required with a finite verb in an ordinary independent statement: parle alone is not the usual way to write I speak. Not every infinitive ending in -er is regular; aller is an important exception. Some otherwise patterned verbs also require spelling adjustments, so the simplest pattern should not be extended blindly to every unfamiliar verb.

## Choosing subjects and making negatives

Tu normally addresses one person in a familiar relationship. Vous addresses more than one person or one person politely; its verb ending stays the same in both uses. On takes a third-person singular verb, even when it informally means we. Standard written negation commonly places ne before the verb and pas after it: je ne parle pas allemand. Ne becomes n' before a vowel sound or a mute h, as in je n'aime pas ce film. Je also contracts to j' in j'aime. Spoken informal French often omits ne, but a beginner writing in a standard formal register should retain both parts unless the task asks for a different register.

## Meaning and paragraph construction

The French present can describe habits or actions happening now; context determines whether English uses a simple or continuous present translation. Je travaille ici can mean I work here or I am working here in an appropriate situation. Add time phrases such as le matin and frequency phrases such as souvent to make the meaning clearer. Connect ideas with et, mais or parce que rather than writing unrelated fragments. A useful paragraph states a routine, adds a relevant detail and explains a preference or contrast. Check each clause separately for subject-verb agreement, then check apostrophes, accents and the placement of negative words. Accuracy in a few well-controlled structures is more valuable than adding long sentences whose meaning you cannot explain.

## Agreement across a connected paragraph

In a short paragraph, each clause needs its own agreement check even when the topic remains the same. Je travaille and mes amis travaillent describe related actions but have different written endings. A coordinated subject that includes the speaker, such as mon frère et moi, normally requires the nous form. Read a coordinated subject as a whole before choosing the ending, rather than matching the verb to the closest noun alone.

Separate grammatical accuracy from whether all requested details have been communicated. A writing task may require a place, a routine and a reason; three accurate but unrelated sentences may still omit part of that task. Use a manageable connector, such as parce que, only when it expresses the intended relationship. Check contractions after adding other words: j'aime becomes je n'aime pas in the negative, because the words immediately next to each other have changed. Standard written forms and informal spoken choices are not interchangeable in every context. If a task specifies formal writing, preserve the expected negative structure and appropriate address.

## Maintaining subject agreement after a contrast

In a connected French description, each new subject controls its own present-tense ending. A contrast word such as mais does not transfer the subject of the first clause into the second. For regular -er verbs, identify the infinitive, remove -er and choose the ending for the stated subject. Written negation surrounds the conjugated verb with ne and pas; ne becomes n’ before a vowel sound. Keep a final proofreading pass for accents and elision separate from the agreement check. The regular pattern does not cover every verb ending in -er: aller is a familiar exception. The examples here deliberately use regular verbs and simple main clauses.

## Negation, elision and agreement with coordinated subjects

In standard written French, ne and pas normally surround the conjugated verb. Before a vowel sound or mute h, ne becomes n'; je also becomes j' before the relevant vowel sound. Elision changes spelling, not the subject or the verb ending. Two named people joined by et take a plural verb. A subject containing moi normally corresponds to nous, while a group containing toi but not moi corresponds to vous. In these exercises the verbs are regular -er verbs; do not extend their endings to every verb family.

## Worked example 1

Complete: Nous ___ le français. Use étudier.

1. Remove -er to obtain étudi-.

2. Add the nous ending -ons.

3. Keep the accent in the stem.

Answer: Nous étudions le français.

## Worked example 2

Make J'aime ce livre negative in standard written French.

1. The negative pattern places ne before the verb and pas after it.

2. Ne contracts to n' before aime.

3. Je appears before n', so use je rather than j' in the completed sentence.

Answer: Je n'aime pas ce livre.

## Worked example 3

Complete 'Mon frère et moi ___ le français' using étudier in the present.

1. The combined subject includes the speaker and another person.

2. Treat it as nous.

3. Use the regular first-person plural form étudions.

Answer: Mon frère et moi étudions le français.

## Worked example 4

Translate into French using étudier and travailler: “We study in the morning, but she does not work on Sundays.”

1. Nous requires the regular -er ending -ons: étudions.

2. Elle requires the ending -e: travaille.

3. Place ne before travaille and pas after it; use mais to preserve the contrast.

Answer: Nous étudions le matin, mais elle ne travaille pas le dimanche.

## Misconceptions

- Vous always refers to several people.
  Correction: It can also address one person politely, with the same verb form.

- Identical pronunciation means identical written verb endings.
  Correction: Parle, parles and parlent can sound alike while agreeing with different subjects.

## Flashcards

### Give the nous and vous forms of parler.
Nous parlons; vous parlez.

### Which verb form follows on in the present?
Third-person singular, as in on parle, even when on means we.

### Where do ne and pas go in a simple present-tense negative?
Around the conjugated verb: Je ne travaille pas ici.

### Write the standard negative of J'aime.
Je n'aime pas.

### Must Je travaille always translate as I work rather than I am working?
No. The surrounding context determines the appropriate English present-tense translation.

### Why recheck contractions after making a French sentence negative?
Adding ne or n' changes which words are adjacent, so the affirmative contraction may no longer appear in the same form.

### Does mais change the subject agreement rule?
No. Each clause’s verb agrees with its own subject.

### What is the regular -er present ending for nous?
-ons, added to the regular stem; check spelling-change exceptions separately.
