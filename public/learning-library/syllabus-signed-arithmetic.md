## Both tiers: order and comparison symbols

On a horizontal number line, numbers increase to the right. Thus −0.8<−0.3 even though 8>3. The symbol ≤ means less than or equal to, ≥ means greater than or equal to, and ≠ means not equal to. Convert fractions to a common denominator or decimals before ordering them. Do not compare just their numerators: 3/4 is greater than 4/7. Equivalent fractions represent the same point, so 2/3=4/6.

A temperature rising from −7°C to 4°C changes by 4−(−7)=11°C. Subtracting a negative adds the opposite. Multiplication and division of two negative numbers give a positive; one negative factor gives a negative product. This sign rule is not an addition rule: −5+(−2)=−7.

## Both tiers: written integer and decimal operations

For column addition or subtraction align place values, including decimal points; insert trailing zeros where helpful. Regroup across a column when necessary. To multiply decimals, first multiply as integers and then restore the total number of decimal places: 2.4×0.35 uses 24×35=840 and three decimal places, so equals 0.840. Check the size: multiplying 2.4 by less than one should reduce it.

Long multiplication separates tens and units: 47×26=47×20+47×6=940+282. For division, make a decimal divisor an integer by multiplying both dividend and divisor by the same power of ten. Thus 7.56÷0.12=756÷12=63. The quotient stays the same because the scale factor cancels. A remainder must be interpreted: 53 people in cars seating 4 need 14 cars, not 13.25 cars.

## Both tiers: exact fraction arithmetic

For addition and subtraction, use equal-sized parts: 2/3+3/4=8/12+9/12=17/12. The denominator is not added. For multiplication multiply numerators and denominators, cancelling common factors across the product first. For division multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor, which must be nonzero. Convert a mixed number to an improper fraction before multiplication or division: 2 1/3=7/3. Keep the sign separate when helpful; −1 1/2 means −3/2, not −1+1/2.

Cancellation removes common factors, not terms separated by addition. In (6×5)/(3×5), cancelling 5 is valid. In (6+5)/(3+5), removing both 5s changes the value. An inverse operation checks a result: if 5/6÷2/3=5/4, then (5/4)×(2/3)=5/6.

## Both tiers: brackets, powers and inverses

Evaluate brackets first, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. Multiplication does not always precede division: 18÷3×2=12. Brackets also distinguish (−3)²=9 from −3²=−9, where the exponent applies only to 3. Use a fraction bar as a grouping symbol for its whole numerator and denominator.

An inverse reverses an operation: addition is reversed by subtraction, multiplication by nonzero division, and a square can be checked by a square root with attention to signs. To check a multi-step answer, work backwards in reverse order or substitute into the original expression. A calculator following correct priority cannot rescue a missing pair of brackets.

## Worked example 1

Evaluate 6.03−2.78 and 3.6×0.24 using written methods.

1. Align hundredths: 603−278=325 hundredths; regroup through the zero tenths.

2. Multiply 36×24=864, then restore three decimal places. Both answers have plausible sizes.

Answer: 3.25 and 0.864.

## Worked example 2

Evaluate (2 1/4−5/6)÷(−17/12).

1. Convert 2 1/4=9/4=27/12 and 5/6=10/12, giving 17/12 in brackets.

2. Multiply (17/12)×(−12/17); cancel nonzero factors to obtain −1.

Answer: −1.

## Misconceptions

- Add fraction denominators when adding fractions.
  Correction: A denominator names the part size. First create equal parts, then add numerators only.

- Multiplication always comes before division.
  Correction: Multiplication and division have equal priority and run left to right unless grouped.

## Flashcards

### Which is larger: −2 or −5?
−2, because it is farther right on the number line.

### How do you divide by a fraction?
Multiply by its reciprocal; the divisor must not be zero.

### What must align in decimal addition?
Place values and decimal points.

### Can you cancel added terms?
No; cancellation requires common multiplicative factors.

### Why is (−4)² different from −4²?
The first squares −4; the second negates the square of 4.

## Practice 4 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Evaluate 8.64÷0.24 without a calculator and check your answer by multiplication.

Answer: 36; 36×0.24=8.64.

1. Scale both numbers by 100 to give 864÷24.

2. 24×30=720 and 24×6=144; together they give 864.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Scale both dividend and divisor.

- Find and check 36.

## Practice 6 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] A temperature changes from 5°C to −9°C, then rises by 6°C. Give the first signed change and the final temperature. Also evaluate (−4)×(−7)÷(−2).

Answer: −14°C change; −3°C final; −14 for the expression.

1. Subtract initial from final for the first change: −9−5=−14.

2. Add 6 to −9. For the expression use 28÷(−2).

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Distinguish change from final temperature.

- Apply the multiplication and division sign rules correctly.

## Practice 7 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Evaluate 20−12÷3×2 and (−5)²−5². Explain why brackets matter.

Answer: 12 and 0.

1. 12÷3×2=8, so the first result is 20−8.

2. The second result is 25−25; brackets make −5 the base of the first square.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Use left-to-right division and multiplication.

- Square the correct base and obtain zero.

## Practice 8 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] A learner cancels the 4s in (8+4)/(2+4) to obtain 4. Correct the result and show a similar expression where cancellation of 4 is valid.

Answer: The sum ratio is 12/6=2. For example (8×4)/(2×4)=8/2=4 permits cancellation.

1. Evaluate each whole sum before dividing.

2. Explain that the cancellation law applies to nonzero common factors, not added terms.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Give 2 for the original expression.

- Give a valid multiplicative example and factor explanation.
