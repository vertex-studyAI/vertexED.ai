## IGCSE both tiers: time units and clocks

There are 60 seconds in a minute, 60 minutes in an hour, 24 hours in a day and 7 days in a week. Use 365 days in a year for these syllabus calculations unless the problem states otherwise. Calendar months have differing lengths, so do not assume one month is exactly four weeks or thirty days. Twelve months make one year.

In the 24-hour clock, 00:00 is midnight and 12:00 is noon. For a p.m. time after noon add 12 to the hour: 7:35 p.m. is 19:35. For a.m. times after midnight keep the hour, so 12:20 a.m. is 00:20. On an analogue clock the long hand shows minutes and the short hand lies partway between hour numbers as minutes pass. A minute hand on 8 represents 40 minutes, not 8 minutes.

## IGCSE both tiers: intervals, timetables and zones

Find elapsed time by counting to a convenient boundary or converting both times to minutes on a common timeline. From 22:45 to 01:20 next day, take 75 minutes to midnight and 80 after midnight, giving 155 minutes. Mark day changes explicitly. A timetable connection is usable only if the next departure is after arrival plus any required transfer time, not merely after the printed hour.

For time zones, convert departure and arrival to one reference offset before subtracting. If city B is three hours ahead of city A, 20:00 in A is 23:00 in B on the same day. A time-zone offset in a question is fixed data; real daylight-saving rules can change and are not assumed. Distinguish a flight duration from the difference between two local clock readings.

## IGCSE both tiers: calculator entry and precision

Convert minutes to a fraction of sixty before entering decimal hours: 2 h 18 min=2.3 h, not 2.18 h. Conversely 3.25 h is 3 h 15 min. Some calculators offer degree-minute-second entry, but a decimal input is interpreted as a decimal unless a different mode is explicitly used. Read the device instructions for keys; mathematical meaning must remain the same.

Use brackets around a whole numerator or denominator. To evaluate (7+5)/(3×8), entering 7+5÷3×8 without grouping gives a different result. Retain intermediate precision using the stored value or answer memory. Round only the final result as requested. Scientific notation such as 4.6E3 means 4600. A display of 4.8 in a money calculation means £4.80, whereas 4.8 hours means 4 h 48 min.

## IGCSE both tiers: money and transaction direction

Track the currency and write final amounts with the appropriate minor unit: £7.5 is conventionally presented as £7.50. A balance changes by adding credits and subtracting debits. If a debit exceeds an available balance, the arithmetic may produce a negative balance; do not silently replace it by zero. Separate unit price, quantity, delivery charge, tax amount and discount according to the wording.

For a stated fictional exchange rate £1=US$1.25, £64 converts to US$80 by multiplication; US$80 converts to £64 by division. If a fixed £2 fee is deducted before conversion, convert £62 instead. If the fee is taken afterwards in dollars, apply it afterwards in dollars. Identifying the operation order is as important as the rate. Check the answer using the inverse operation and an estimate.

## Worked example 1

A train arrives at 23:48 and the connecting train leaves at 00:17 next day. How long is the connection? Is it valid if a 25-minute transfer is required?

1. From 23:48 to midnight is 12 minutes, then another 17 minutes.

2. Compare 29 with the required 25, not just the clock-hour numbers.

Answer: 29 minutes; yes, with 4 minutes to spare.

## Worked example 2

A £90 exchange has a £3 fee deducted first. Use fictional rate £1=€1.18. What is received?

1. The amount converted is 90−3=£87.

2. 87×1.18=€102.66; keep the fee in its stated currency and position.

Answer: €102.66.

## Misconceptions

- 2 h 45 min is 2.45 h.
  Correction: It is 2+45/60=2.75 h.

- Subtract local arrival and departure times to find every flight duration.
  Correction: First place both on one time-zone timeline and account for dates.

## Practice 1 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Convert 2.4 days to hours, 0.35 hours to minutes and 540 seconds to minutes. How many days are in two years using the syllabus convention?

Answer: 57.6 h; 21 min; 9 min; 730 days.

1. Multiply days by 24 and hours by 60; divide seconds by 60.

2. Use 365 days per year as specified.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Give all three unit conversions.

- Use the stated year convention.

## Practice 2 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Write 12:15 a.m. and 8:07 p.m. in 24-hour notation. An analogue minute hand points to 7 and the hour hand is between 3 and 4; give the time.

Answer: 00:15; 20:07; 3:35.

1. Midnight-hour times use hour 00; add 12 for 8 p.m.

2. Seven five-minute divisions give 35 minutes past 3.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Convert both digital times.

- Read the analogue-hand description.

## Practice 3 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] A coach leaves at 21:56 and arrives at 02:14 the next day. Find its duration.

Answer: 4 h 18 min.

1. From 21:56 to midnight is 2 h 4 min.

2. Add the 2 h 14 min after midnight.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Account for midnight.

- Give 4 h 18 min.

## Practice 4 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] A train arrives at 14:47. Connections depart at 14:50, 15:02 and 15:20. A transfer requires at least 12 minutes. Which is the earliest usable connection and what is the wait?

Answer: 15:02; 15 minutes.

1. Earliest permissible departure is 14:59.

2. Select the first listed departure at or after that time and subtract 14:47.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Apply the transfer requirement.

- Select 15:02 and a 15-minute wait.

## Practice 5 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] A flight leaves city A (UTC+2) at 22:30 on Monday and arrives in city B (UTC+5) at 06:10 on Tuesday. Using these fixed offsets, find duration.

Answer: 4 h 40 min.

1. Departure is Monday 20:30 UTC and arrival is Tuesday 01:10 UTC.

2. Count 3 h 30 min to midnight and 1 h 10 min afterwards.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Convert both times to a common offset.

- Handle the date crossing to obtain 4 h 40 min.

## Practice 6 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Evaluate (13.7+2.9)/(4.2−1.6) to three significant figures. Explain how you would enter it and why 2.6 must not be rounded to 3 first.

Answer: 6.38; enter both numerator and denominator in brackets.

1. The exact decimal numerator is 16.6 and denominator 2.6, so the quotient is 6.384615… .

2. Rounding the denominator prematurely changes the calculation rather than just the final reporting.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Use correct grouped entry and retain precision.

- Round the final result to 6.38.

## Practice 7 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Interpret a display of 5.75 as hours and separately as pounds. Convert 1 h 42 min to a decimal-hour calculator input.

Answer: 5 h 45 min; £5.75; 1.7 h.

1. Multiply the fractional hour 0.75 by 60.

2. Use 42/60=0.7 rather than entering 1.42.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Interpret both units correctly.

- Give 1.7 h.

## Practice 8 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] A balance is £46.80. A £75 credit is followed by debits of £28.45 and £99. Find the final balance and explain its sign.

Answer: −£5.65; the debits exceed the available credited balance.

1. 46.80+75=121.80.

2. Subtract 28.45 and then 99 to obtain −5.65.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Apply credit and debit directions.

- Give the signed result with an interpretation.

## Practice 9 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Using fictional rate £1=US$1.28, convert US$192 to pounds. If a £4 fee is then deducted, what remains?

Answer: £150 before the fee; £146 afterwards.

1. Divide dollars by 1.28 to reverse the pounds-to-dollars rate.

2. Subtract the fee in pounds after conversion.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Use the inverse conversion.

- Deduct the fee at the stated stage.
