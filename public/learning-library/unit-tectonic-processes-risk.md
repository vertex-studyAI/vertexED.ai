## Moving plates and different boundaries

The lithosphere consists of crust and rigid uppermost mantle and is divided into moving plates. At constructive or divergent boundaries, plates separate and magma can rise and solidify to form new crust. At destructive subduction boundaries, an oceanic plate descends beneath another plate; melting processes above the descending slab can supply magma, while locked interfaces can store strain. At continental collision zones, buoyant crust shortens and thickens, building mountains without the typical volcanic arc of oceanic subduction. At conservative or transform boundaries, plates slide past one another, so earthquakes occur without systematic creation or destruction of crust.

## Earthquakes and their effects

A fault can remain locked while surrounding rock deforms. Sudden slip releases stored energy as seismic waves. The focus is the location within the Earth where rupture begins; the epicentre is the point directly above it at the surface. Magnitude describes an event’s size, while intensity describes observed shaking effects at a location. These are not interchangeable. Collapsing buildings are a direct effect; disrupted water supplies and disease risk may follow as secondary consequences. Some undersea earthquakes displace the seabed and generate tsunamis, but not every earthquake does so.

## Risk is not magnitude alone

Exposure concerns people and assets in the affected area; vulnerability concerns their susceptibility to harm and capacity to cope. Building design, enforcement, population density, depth, local ground conditions, time of day and access for rescue can all affect losses. Wealth can support stronger infrastructure and response, but it is not a universal explanation on its own. People may remain in hazardous areas because of work, family, limited alternatives, fertile soils or geothermal opportunities. Calling residents irrational ignores constraints and benefits.

## Management and evidence

Protection such as appropriately engineered buildings can reduce collapse; planning, drills and reliable communication can improve response. Monitoring helps identify volcanic unrest, but precise earthquake prediction of time, place and magnitude is not reliably available. Early warning detects an earthquake that has already started and may provide seconds before strong shaking arrives elsewhere; it is not prediction before rupture.

For an original fictional comparison, Settlement A has 20000 exposed residents and 20 deaths; B has 100000 exposed residents and 50 deaths. A has fewer deaths in total but a higher death rate among exposed residents: 0.10% versus 0.05%. Even this comparison cannot isolate the effect of wealth without comparable shaking, buildings and other conditions. Real required named examples need checked place-specific evidence rather than replacing their data with these invented figures.

## Worked example 1

Why are conservative margins associated with earthquakes but not a continuous volcanic arc?

1. Identify horizontal relative motion and locking.

2. Distinguish the earthquake mechanism from magma-generation conditions.

Answer: Fault slip releases strain, but the boundary does not systematically create magma through subduction or divergent upwelling.

## Worked example 2

Use the fictional A/B data to compare absolute deaths and risk among exposed residents.

1. 20/20000 × 100 = 0.10%; 50/100000 × 100 = 0.05%.

2. Do not infer a single cause from two uncontrolled cases.

Answer: B has more deaths, but A’s rate is twice B’s.

## Misconceptions

- Every convergent boundary produces volcanoes.
  Correction: Continental collision differs from oceanic subduction and does not normally create the same volcanic arc.

- Early warning predicts an earthquake before it begins.
  Correction: It detects a rupture already in progress and can warn more distant locations before stronger waves arrive.
