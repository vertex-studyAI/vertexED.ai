## Label relative to the angle

The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle and is the longest side of a right triangle. Opposite and adjacent depend on the acute angle selected: changing the reference angle swaps those two labels, not the hypotenuse. In a right triangle, sine is opposite divided by hypotenuse, cosine is adjacent divided by hypotenuse, and tangent is opposite divided by adjacent. First identify the given and required sides, then select a ratio that connects them. An unlabeled sketch should not be measured as if it were a scale drawing.

## Rearrange and choose the angle unit

If sin θ=opposite/hypotenuse, multiply by the hypotenuse to recover the opposite length. If the hypotenuse is unknown, divide the opposite length by sin θ. For an unknown acute angle, apply the appropriate inverse trigonometric function to a dimensionless ratio. A degree question requires degree mode; radians are a different unit for the same angle. Keep extra digits during working and round the final value to the requested precision. A hypotenuse shorter than a leg signals an error that rounding cannot explain.

## Model the geometry explicitly

A bearing is measured clockwise from north at the stated starting point. North lines at different locations are parallel in a local plane model, but the direction from A to B is not the direction from B to A. Draw the north line and identify the right triangle before choosing a ratio. Horizontal distance, line-of-sight distance and vertical height are different lengths. A height measured from an observer's eye level may need the observer's eye height added to obtain height above the ground. State idealisations such as level ground.

## Worked example 1

A right triangle has hypotenuse 10 cm and an acute angle of 30°. Find the opposite side.

1. Use opposite=10 sin30°.

2. Since sin30°=1/2, the opposite side is half the hypotenuse.

Answer: 5 cm.

## Worked example 2

A point lies 6 km east and 8 km north of a starting point. Find the straight-line distance.

1. The east and north displacements are perpendicular.

2. Apply Pythagoras: d²=6²+8²=100.

Answer: 10 km.

## Misconceptions

- The adjacent side is the hypotenuse.
  Correction: In the cosine/tangent labels, adjacent means the non-hypotenuse leg next to the angle.

- Every triangle permits direct use of these right-triangle ratios.
  Correction: A right angle must be present or constructed before applying this form.

## Practice 1 — standard · 2 marks

In a right triangle, the side opposite 30° is 7 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

Answer: 14 cm.

1. Use sin30°=7/h.

2. Rearrange h=7/(1/2).

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Choose sine.

- Find 14 cm.

## Practice 2 — standard · 2 marks

The opposite and adjacent legs relative to θ are both 9 cm. Find the acute angle θ.

Answer: 45°.

1. tan θ=9/9=1.

2. The acute angle with tangent one is 45°.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Form the ratio one.

- State 45°.

## Practice 3 — standard · 2 marks

The bearing of B from A is 065°. In a local plane model, find the bearing of A from B.

Answer: 245°.

1. Reverse the direction by adding 180°.

2. The result is below 360°, so no further adjustment is needed.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Reverse direction by 180°.

- Give 245°.

## Practice 4 — extension · 2 marks

On level ground, an observer's eye is 1.6 m above the ground and 12 m horizontally from a vertical pole. The top has elevation 45°. Find the pole's height.

Answer: 13.6 m.

1. The rise above eye level is 12 tan45°=12 m.

2. Add the eye height 1.6 m.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Find the rise above eye level.

- Add the reference height.
