## Both tiers: standard units and conversion factors

Use units to name the measured quantity: metres for length, kilograms for mass, seconds for time and a stated currency for money. A conversion changes the numerical representation, not the physical amount. There are 1000 mm in 1 m, 100 cm in 1 m, 1000 m in 1 km, 1000 g in 1 kg and 1000 kg in 1 tonne. Multiply when changing to smaller units and divide when changing to larger units, but justify the factor rather than memorising a direction alone.

Area and volume factors must also be squared or cubed. Since 1 m=100 cm, 1 m²=10,000 cm² and 1 m³=1,000,000 cm³. One litre is 1000 mL=1000 cm³, and 1 m³ is 1000 litres. Do not apply a length factor directly to an area. When an imperial conversion is needed, use the supplied relation, for example 1 mile≈1.6 km; the approximation limits the precision of the result.

## IGCSE both tiers: pay, exchange, flow and fuel

A rate compares quantities with different units. At £12 per hour, 3.5 hours earns £42 before any other terms. If £1 buys €1.20, multiply pounds by 1.20 to obtain euros; divide euros by 1.20 to reverse the conversion. Writing the units beside the fraction makes the direction clear. A fictional exchange rate here is a problem datum, not a current market quote.

A tap delivering 8 litres per minute fills 40 litres in 40/8=5 minutes if its rate is constant. Fuel consumption may be expressed as kilometres per litre or litres per 100 kilometres; these are different ratios. For 6 L per 100 km over 250 km, fuel=6×250/100=15 L. A larger km/L figure means better economy, whereas a smaller L/100 km figure does. If a question includes a fixed fee or a changing rate, calculate each component or interval separately.

## IGCSE both tiers: supplied compound-rate formulas

For density use mass/volume; for pressure use force/area; for population density use population/land area. These less familiar rate formulas are supplied in the IGCSE Number questions here. Substitute consistent units, then interpret the quotient. A 90 g sample occupying 30 cm³ has density 3 g/cm³. To recover mass, multiply density by volume; check that the volume units cancel.

A city with 24,000 people over 12 km² has mean population density 2000 people/km². This is a spatial average, not a claim that every square kilometre contains exactly 2000 people. Similarly, pressure calculated using total force and area assumes the relevant mean distribution. Describe assumptions when applying a mathematical rate to a real situation.

## Both tiers: speed and total-time averages

Know speed=distance/time, distance=speed×time and time=distance/speed. A speed in km/h requires hours, so 2 h 24 min=2+24/60=2.4 h. To convert m/s to km/h multiply by 3.6 because there are 3600 seconds per hour and 1000 metres per kilometre; divide by 3.6 in the reverse direction.

Average speed over a journey is total distance divided by total elapsed time, including stops if the question includes them. The arithmetic mean of two speeds works only for equal time intervals, not generally for equal distances. Travelling 60 km at 30 km/h and returning 60 km at 60 km/h takes 2+1=3 h, so the average is 120/3=40 km/h, not 45 km/h. State whether waiting time is included before dividing.

## Worked example 1

Convert 2.4 m² to cm² and 0.006 m³ to litres.

1. Square the length factor: 2.4×100²=24,000.

2. Use 1000 litres per cubic metre: 0.006×1000=6.

Answer: 24,000 cm² and 6 litres.

## Worked example 2

A vehicle travels 72 km in 1 h 12 min, waits 18 min, then travels 45 km in 45 min. Find its overall average speed including the stop.

1. Total distance=117 km; total elapsed time=72+18+45=135 min=2.25 h.

2. Average speed=117/2.25=52 km/h; do not average segment speeds.

Answer: 52 km/h.

## Misconceptions

- Convert m² to cm² by multiplying by 100.
  Correction: Each of the two dimensions gains a factor 100, so the area factor is 10,000.

- Average speed is always the mean of the listed speeds.
  Correction: Use total distance divided by total elapsed time; unequal time intervals need weighting.

## Practice 1 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Convert 0.85 tonnes to kg, 3.2 km to m, and 450 mL to litres.

Answer: 850 kg, 3200 m, 0.45 L.

1. Use 1000 kg per tonne and 1000 m per km.

2. Use 1000 mL per litre, dividing for the last conversion.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Convert mass and length correctly.

- Convert capacity correctly.

## Practice 2 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Convert 7500 cm² to m² and 320,000 cm³ to m³. Explain the different divisors.

Answer: 0.75 m² and 0.32 m³.

1. Divide area by 100²=10000.

2. Divide volume by 100³=1000000.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Give both values.

- Explain squared versus cubed conversion factors.

## Practice 3 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher] Using the supplied approximation 1 mile=1.6 km, convert 18 miles to km and 56 km to miles.

Answer: 28.8 km and 35 miles, using the given approximation.

1. Multiply 18 by 1.6.

2. Divide 56 by 1.6 for the reverse direction.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Use the forward conversion correctly.

- Use the reverse conversion correctly.

## Practice 4 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] A worker earns £14 per hour for 7 h 30 min. Convert the earnings to euros using the fictional rate £1=€1.16.

Answer: £105; €121.80.

1. 7 h 30 min=7.5 h; 14×7.5=105.

2. 105×1.16=121.8 euros, written to two decimal places.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Calculate earnings from decimal hours.

- Apply the exchange direction and money formatting.

## Practice 5 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] At 12 L/min how long does filling 90 L take? A separate vehicle uses 7.2 L per 100 km; how much fuel does a 350 km trip use?

Answer: 7.5 minutes; 25.2 L.

1. Filling time=volume/rate=90/12.

2. Fuel=7.2×350/100.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Use the inverse flow relation.

- Interpret litres per 100 km correctly.

## Practice 6 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Use density=mass/volume and pressure=force/area. A 156 g object occupies 60 cm³. A 480 N force acts on 0.12 m². Calculate density and pressure.

Answer: 2.6 g/cm³ and 4000 N/m².

1. 156/60=2.6, retaining mass per volume units.

2. 480/0.12=4000, retaining force per area units.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Calculate and label density.

- Calculate and label pressure.

## Practice 7 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Using population density=population/area, find the mean density of 63,000 people in 18 km². Does it prove every km² has that many people?

Answer: 3500 people/km²; no, the population can be unevenly distributed.

1. Divide 63000 by 18.

2. Distinguish a mean across the whole area from a uniform local distribution.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Calculate the rate with units.

- Explain the averaging limitation.

## Practice 8 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] A runner covers 9 km in 48 min. Find average speed in km/h and convert 5 m/s to km/h.

Answer: 11.25 km/h and 18 km/h.

1. 48/60=0.8 h and 9/0.8=11.25.

2. 5×3600/1000=18.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Convert time before dividing.

- Use the correct speed conversion factor.

## Practice 9 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] A cyclist travels 24 km at 12 km/h and another 24 km at 24 km/h. Find average speed over the full trip with no stops.

Answer: 16 km/h.

1. The legs take 2 h and 1 h.

2. Divide the total 48 km by the total 3 h.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Find the two times rather than averaging speeds.

- Give 16 km/h.

## Practice 10 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Use density=mass/volume. A liquid has density 0.8 g/cm³ and volume 250 cm³. Find its mass in grams and kg.

Answer: 200 g=0.2 kg.

1. Rearrange to mass=density×volume.

2. Multiply 0.8×250=200, then divide grams by 1000.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Recover mass using multiplication.

- Convert correctly to kilograms.
