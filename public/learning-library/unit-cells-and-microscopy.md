## Common structures: GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended

A cell membrane regulates movement between a cell and its surroundings. Cytoplasm contains the sites of many chemical reactions. Ribosomes assemble proteins. In a typical animal or plant cell, a nucleus contains genetic information and mitochondria support aerobic respiration. These are functional relationships: saying that a cell has many mitochondria is incomplete until you connect this with an energy-demanding process.

Plant cells also have a cellulose wall that supports the cell. A large permanent vacuole containing cell sap helps maintain pressure against that wall. Chloroplasts absorb light for photosynthesis, but not every plant cell contains them: an underground root cell usually does not. A bacterial cell has a membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes and a wall, but no membrane-bound nucleus; its main DNA is circular and it may contain plasmids.

## Specialisation and organisation: both tiers

New cells arise from existing cells. Differentiation produces cells with structures suited to particular roles. A root hair increases the surface available for absorption; palisade cells contain many chloroplasts; ciliated airway cells move mucus. Red blood cells carry oxygen using haemoglobin, while neurones transmit electrical impulses. Sperm and egg cells are gametes involved in reproduction. These descriptions identify roles, not conscious intentions.

A tissue is a group of cells working together. An organ contains different tissues that cooperate, such as the muscular and glandular tissues in a stomach. Several organs form a system, and systems operate within an organism. A cell, tissue and organ are not interchangeable levels of description.

## Images, scale and resolution: both tiers

Magnification equals image length divided by actual length, with both lengths in the same unit. A 24 mm drawing of a 0.08 mm cell has magnification 300. Magnification has no length unit. Resolution is the ability to distinguish two nearby points as separate: enlarging a blurred image does not reveal new detail. Start a school microscope on low power, locate and focus the specimen, then change magnification under supervision. Draw clear outlines and labels with a scale or stated magnification, not decorative shading.

## Unit conversion: GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Extended only

One millimetre equals 1000 micrometres. Convert before dividing: 18 mm equals 18000 micrometres, so an 18 mm image of a 60 micrometre cell is magnified 300 times. Dividing 18 by 60 without conversion gives a result smaller by a factor of 1000. A scale bar stays meaningful when an image is resized because the bar and specimen enlarge together; a printed magnification label may no longer be valid.

## Worked example 1

A 30 mm image represents a cell 0.05 mm long. Find magnification.

1. Both lengths are in millimetres.

2. Divide 30 by 0.05; the units cancel.

Answer: 600 times.

## Worked example 2

Why does a root hair cell lack chloroplasts but have a long projection?

1. Link the projection to increased surface area for uptake.

2. Distinguish the root function from the light-absorbing role of a leaf cell.

Answer: The projection supports absorption; underground light conditions do not support photosynthesis.

## Misconceptions

- Every plant cell photosynthesises.
  Correction: Many plant cells, including ordinary underground root hair cells, lack chloroplasts.

- A larger picture always has better resolution.
  Correction: Magnification increases apparent size; resolution determines whether nearby details can be distinguished.

## Practice 1 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Explain why a muscle cell often has many mitochondria.

Answer: Contraction needs energy; aerobic respiration in mitochondria supports this demand.

1. Identify contraction as the function.

2. Connect mitochondria with aerobic respiration, not with storing all energy.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Energy is needed for contraction.

- Mitochondria are sites of aerobic respiration.

## Practice 2 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Give two structural differences between a typical bacterial cell and a typical leaf cell.

Answer: The bacterium lacks a membrane-bound nucleus and chloroplasts.

1. Compare corresponding structures rather than only size.

2. A leaf cell can enclose DNA in a nucleus and contain chloroplasts.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- No membrane-bound bacterial nucleus.

- No bacterial chloroplasts.

## Practice 3 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] A specimen is 0.12 mm long and its image is 48 mm long. Calculate magnification.

Answer: 400 times.

1. Use matching millimetre units.

2. 48 / 0.12 = 400.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Correct division.

- 400 without a length unit.

## Practice 4 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] A blurred micrograph is enlarged tenfold. Explain why two merged cell boundaries may remain merged.

Answer: Enlargement does not add resolving information.

1. Define resolution as distinguishing close points.

2. The existing blur is enlarged along with the image.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Resolution distinguished from magnification.

- No new detail created.

## Practice 5 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Extended only] An image is 36 mm wide and the actual cell is 45 micrometres wide. Calculate magnification.

Answer: 800 times.

1. 36 mm = 36000 micrometres.

2. 36000 / 45 = 800.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Correct conversion.

- 800 times.

## Practice 6 — standard · 3 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Place muscle cell, stomach, digestive system and muscle tissue in increasing organisational scale, explaining the two middle levels.

Answer: Muscle cell, muscle tissue, stomach, digestive system.

1. Similar cells cooperate in tissue.

2. A stomach combines different tissues; organs cooperate in a system.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Correct order.

- Tissue explanation.

- Organ explanation.
