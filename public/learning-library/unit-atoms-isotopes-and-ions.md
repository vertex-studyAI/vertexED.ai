## Particles and identity: both tiers

The nucleus contains protons with relative charge +1 and neutrons with charge 0. Electrons have relative charge -1 and occupy regions outside the nucleus. A proton and a neutron each have relative mass approximately 1, while an electron has a much smaller mass. Most atomic mass is therefore concentrated in the tiny nucleus, although most of the atom is the surrounding region.

Atomic number counts protons and identifies the element. Mass number counts protons plus neutrons in a particular atom. For sodium-23 with atomic number 11, there are 11 protons and 12 neutrons. A neutral atom has an equal number of electrons and protons. A mass number is not the decimal relative atomic mass printed for many elements.

## Ions and isotopes: both tiers

An ion forms when electrons are gained or lost; its nucleus does not change in this process. Sodium forms Na+ by losing one electron, so Na+ has 11 protons and 10 electrons. Chloride, Cl-, has one more electron than the neutral chlorine atom. A positive ion is not formed by adding protons.

Isotopes share a proton number but differ in neutron number. Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 are therefore the same element with different nuclear masses. Interpret the notation carefully: a 2+ charge tells you to subtract two electrons from the neutral count, not two neutrons from the mass number.

## Electron arrangements: both tiers

For the first twenty elements, the school shell model fills 2 electrons in the first shell and then up to 8 in each of the next shells before potassium and calcium begin the fourth shell. Magnesium has arrangement 2,8,2 and its ion Mg2+ has 2,8. The period gives the number of occupied shells in the atom. Main-group outer electrons connect to group position; helium is the full-shell exception with only two electrons. This simplified model should not be extrapolated blindly to transition elements.

## Isotope means: GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Extended only

A relative atomic mass is a weighted mean on the carbon-12 scale, not the mass number of one atom. Multiply each isotope mass by its fractional abundance and add. For 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37, the mean is 35.5. It must lie between 35 and 37 and nearer the more abundant isotope. Isotopes of an element have the same electron arrangement in neutral atoms, so their chemical behaviour is very similar; different neutron numbers do not rearrange the outer electrons.

## Worked example 1

Find the particles in aluminium-27, atomic number 13, as Al3+.

1. Neutrons = 27 - 13 = 14.

2. A 3+ ion has lost three of the neutral atom’s 13 electrons.

Answer: 13 protons, 14 neutrons, 10 electrons.

## Worked example 2

An element has 80% mass-10 and 20% mass-11 atoms. Find its mean relative atomic mass.

1. Weight the masses: 10 × 0.80 + 11 × 0.20.

2. 8 + 2.2 = 10.2, closer to the more abundant mass-10 isotope.

Answer: 10.2.

## Misconceptions

- Isotopes are different elements.
  Correction: Their proton number is identical; only neutron count differs.

- Ion charge changes mass number.
  Correction: Ordinary ion formation transfers electrons; the proton and neutron counts remain unchanged.

## Flashcards

### What identifies an element?
Its proton number.

### How are neutrons counted?
Mass number minus proton number.

### How does a positive ion form?
Loss of electrons.

### What differs between isotopes?
Neutron number.

### Why is a relative atomic mass sometimes decimal?
It is an abundance-weighted mean, not a single mass number.

## Practice 1 — standard · 3 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] For magnesium-24 with atomic number 12, state the particle counts in Mg2+.

Answer: 12 protons, 12 neutrons, 10 electrons.

1. Subtract atomic number from mass number.

2. Subtract the positive charge from the neutral electron count.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- 12 protons.

- 12 neutrons.

- 10 electrons.

## Practice 2 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Chlorine-37 has atomic number 17. How many electrons and neutrons are in Cl-?

Answer: 18 electrons and 20 neutrons.

1. Negative charge means one electron gained.

2. 37 - 17 = 20 neutrons.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- 18 electrons.

- 20 neutrons.

## Practice 3 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] An atom has electron arrangement 2,8,7. Give its period and its Cambridge main-group number, explaining both.

Answer: Period 3 and Group VII.

1. Count three occupied shells.

2. Count seven outer-shell electrons; this is modern Group 17.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Period 3 with shells reason.

- Group VII with outer-electron reason.

## Practice 4 — standard · 3 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Carbon-12 and carbon-14 each have six protons. Explain why they are isotopes, giving their neutron counts.

Answer: They are the same element, with six and eight neutrons respectively.

1. Same proton number defines carbon.

2. Subtract six from each mass number.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Same element.

- Different neutron counts.

- 6 and 8 neutrons.

## Practice 5 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Extended only] A sample contains 60% mass-20 isotope and 40% mass-22 isotope. Find the relative atomic mass.

Answer: 20.8.

1. 20 × 0.60 + 22 × 0.40.

2. 12 + 8.8 = 20.8.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Correct weighting.

- 20.8.

## Practice 6 — extension · 2 marks

[IGCSE Extended only] Explain why chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 have similar chemical properties despite their different masses.

Answer: Their neutral atoms have the same electron arrangement and outer-shell electrons.

1. Both have 17 protons and therefore 17 electrons when neutral.

2. Chemical reactions involve electrons rather than changing neutron number.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Same electron arrangement.

- Outer electrons linked to chemical behaviour.
