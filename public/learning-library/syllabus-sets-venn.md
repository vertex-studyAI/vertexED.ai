## IGCSE both tiers: universal set, complement and set definitions

A set is a collection of distinct elements. Order in a written list is irrelevant and repetitions do not create new elements. The universal set U states the allowed universe. With U={1,2,3,4,5,6}, define A={2,4,6}, the even members. Its complement A′={1,3,5} contains elements of U not in A. Changing U can change A′, so always identify the universe.

n(A) is the number of elements, here 3. A set-builder description gives a rule: B={x: x is an integer and 1≤x<4} means B={1,2,3}. The endpoints matter: the inequality excludes 4. A finite list and a rule can describe the same set, but the rule must include the intended number type; all real numbers between 1 and 4 would be an infinite set instead.

## IGCSE both tiers: two-set construction and counting

A∩B is the intersection: elements in both. A∪B is the union: elements in at least one, including those in both. Draw a rectangle for U and two overlapping circles A and B. The four disjoint regions are A only, A∩B, B only and outside both. Place each element in exactly one region. To shade A∩B shade just the lens; to shade A∪B shade both complete circles; to shade (A∪B)′ shade the rectangle outside both circles.

When totals are given, fill the overlap first. With 20 students, 12 in A, 9 in B and 5 in both, the A-only region has 12−5=7 and the B-only region 9−5=4. The union contains 7+5+4=16, leaving 4 outside. Equivalently n(A∪B)=n(A)+n(B)−n(A∩B); subtraction removes the overlap counted twice. Core Venn diagrams are limited to two sets.

## IGCSE Extended: membership and subsets

The notation x∈A means x is an element of A; x∉A means it is not. The empty set ∅ contains no elements. If every element of A belongs to B, then A is a subset of B, written A⊆B here; A⊈B means at least one element of A is outside B. Some specifications and questions use ⊂ for subset: follow the convention stated in the question, especially if strict containment is relevant. Membership compares an element with a set, while subset compares two sets.

For U={1,2,3,4}, A={2,4} and B={1,2,3,4}, we have 2∈A and A⊆B, but {2} is a subset rather than the number 2 itself. If two sets have no shared elements their intersection is ∅; that does not make either set empty. Since the empty set has no counterexample element, it is a subset of every set.

## IGCSE Extended: three-set regions

Three overlapping circles split the universe into eight disjoint regions: A only, B only, C only, A-and-B only, A-and-C only, B-and-C only, all three and outside all. Put the triple intersection in first. If n(A∩B)=8 and the triple intersection contains 3, then A-and-B only contains 5, because the pairwise intersection includes the triple region unless the question says only.

After the three pair-only regions, use each set total to obtain its single-only region. Add all seven internal regions once to find the union, then subtract from the universe for the outside region. The formula n(A∪B∪C)=n(A)+n(B)+n(C)−n(A∩B)−n(A∩C)−n(B∩C)+n(A∩B∩C) follows the same count correction. Re-add the regions within each original circle as a check. Negative region counts indicate inconsistent data or double subtraction.

## Worked example 1

Let U={1,…,10}, A be multiples of 2 and B be multiples of 3. Describe all four Venn regions.

1. List A={2,4,6,8,10} and B={3,6,9}. Place the common element 6 first.

2. Remove 6 from each list for the single-only regions, then find unused members of U.

Answer: A only={2,4,8,10}; overlap={6}; B only={3,9}; outside={1,5,7}.

## Worked example 2

[Extended] In a group of 40, n(A)=20, n(B)=18, n(C)=15, n(A∩B)=8, n(A∩C)=7, n(B∩C)=6 and n(A∩B∩C)=3. Find all regions.

1. Subtract the triple 3 from each pair total to get 5,4,3.

2. Subtract each circle’s pair-only and triple counts from its total, obtaining 8,7,5.

3. The seven inside regions sum to 35; subtract from 40.

Answer: Triple=3; AB only=5; AC only=4; BC only=3; A only=8; B only=7; C only=5; outside=5.

## Misconceptions

- Union excludes the overlap.
  Correction: Union means at least one set and includes the overlap.

- A pairwise intersection in a three-set question means exactly two sets.
  Correction: It includes the triple intersection unless the word only or an equivalent exclusion is stated.

## Practice 1 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Let U={1,2,…,12} and A={x: x is an integer, 3≤x<8}. List A, A′ and n(A).

Answer: A={3,4,5,6,7}; A′={1,2,8,9,10,11,12}; n(A)=5.

1. Include the lower boundary 3 and exclude 8.

2. Take the complement only within the stated U.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- List A and its count correctly.

- List the complete complement.

## Practice 2 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Draw two overlapping circles inside U={1,…,8}. Put A={1,2,4,8} and B={2,3,5,8} into the four regions. List A∩B and A∪B.

Answer: A only={1,4}; overlap={2,8}; B only={3,5}; outside={6,7}. Intersection={2,8}; union={1,2,3,4,5,8}.

1. Place 2 and 8 in the overlap first.

2. Place every remaining member once, then read intersection and union from the regions.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Place all elements in correct disjoint regions.

- List intersection and union correctly.

## Practice 3 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] On a two-circle Venn diagram describe precisely which region to shade for A∩B′ and for (A∪B)′.

Answer: A∩B′ is A only; (A∪B)′ is outside both circles but inside U.

1. A∩B′ requires being in A and not in B.

2. The complement of the union excludes every point in either circle.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Identify A only.

- Identify the outside-both region within U.

## Practice 4 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Among 35 learners, 19 study French, 16 study German and 7 study both. Find the counts for French only, German only and neither.

Answer: 12, 9 and 7.

1. Subtract the overlap from each set total.

2. The union has 19+16−7=28, leaving 35−28=7.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Give both single-only counts.

- Give the neither count without double counting.

## Practice 5 — extension · 2 marks

[IGCSE Extended] For U={1,2,3,4,5}, A={2,4} and B={2,3,4}, decide whether 2∈A, 5∈B, A⊆B and B⊆A are true. Find A∩{1,3,5}.

Answer: True, false, true, false; the intersection is ∅.

1. 2 is an element of A; 5 is not an element of B.

2. Every A element is in B, but 3 in B is not in A; no A element is odd.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Assess the membership and subset statements correctly.

- Give the empty intersection and explain the counterexample to B⊆A.

## Practice 6 — extension · 3 marks

[IGCSE Extended] Draw three-set Venn regions for a group of 50 with totals A=24, B=22, C=20; pairwise intersections AB=10, AC=9, BC=8; triple=4. Find exactly one set, exactly two sets and none.

Answer: Exactly one=24; exactly two=15; none=7. Regions: A only=9, B only=8, C only=7, AB only=6, AC only=5, BC only=4, triple=4.

1. Fill triple 4, then pair-only counts 6,5,4.

2. Deduct these from set totals to find singles 9,8,7; all internal regions total 43.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Calculate all pair-only and single-only regions.

- Sum exactly-one and exactly-two counts separately.

- Find 7 outside all sets.

## Practice 7 — extension · 2 marks

[IGCSE Extended] Describe the regions shaded by (A∩B)∩C′ and by (A∪B∪C)′ in a three-set Venn diagram.

Answer: The AB-only lens excluding C; the outside-all region within U.

1. The first expression requires A and B membership but excludes every C member.

2. The second excludes the entire union of the three circles.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Exclude the triple region from the first shading.

- Identify the complement of the three-set union.

## Revision guide
