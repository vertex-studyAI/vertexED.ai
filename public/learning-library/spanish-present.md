# Regular present-tense verbs and subject agreement

## Prerequisites

- Recognise the subject of a sentence.

- Recognise infinitives ending in -ar, -er and -ir.

## Objectives

- Form regular present-tense verbs for different subjects.

- Interpret routine statements using context.

- Write a short connected account with agreement.

## Finding the stem and selecting an ending

A Spanish infinitive names an action without specifying the subject: hablar means to speak, comer means to eat and vivir means to live. For these regular verbs, remove -ar, -er or -ir to find the stem, then add the ending for the subject. Hablar becomes hablo, hablas, habla, hablamos, habláis and hablan for yo, tú, él/ella/usted, nosotros/nosotras, vosotros/vosotras and ellos/ellas/ustedes. Comer uses como, comes, come, comemos, coméis and comen. Vivir uses vivo, vives, vive, vivimos, vivís and viven. Written accents in forms such as habláis are part of correct spelling. These patterns describe regular verbs only: they do not justify forms such as yo sabo for the irregular verb saber.

## Subject, audience and meaning

A verb ending often identifies enough about the subject that an explicit subject pronoun is unnecessary. Hablo español can mean I speak Spanish without adding yo. Include a pronoun when it clarifies a contrast or removes ambiguity. Usted refers to the person being addressed politely but takes the same verb form as él or ella. Ustedes takes the ellos/ellas form and is the usual plural you across much of the Spanish-speaking world; vosotros/vosotras is widely used for familiar plural address in Spain. The present tense can express a habit or a present situation, and sometimes a scheduled future event with suitable context. An isolated form does not automatically determine every nuance of its English translation.

## Building and checking a short account

Start with a clear subject, choose the appropriate verb and then add details of time, place or frequency. Estudio por la tarde means I study in the afternoon; normalmente estudio por la tarde adds frequency. Put no before the conjugated verb for a simple negative: no estudio aquí. Connect related statements with y or contrast them with pero, making sure each verb still agrees with its own subject. Mi hermana estudia y yo trabajo has two subjects and therefore two different endings. When checking your writing, underline each subject and match it to its verb before checking spelling and accents. Do not translate English word order mechanically: learn useful phrases in context and distinguish a language's normal expression from a word-for-word gloss.

## From isolated forms to purposeful communication

A successful beginner response communicates the requested information in a controlled grammatical form. Before writing, identify who performs each action, whether the statement is positive or negative, and which time or frequency detail is needed. A phrase such as todos los días expresses a repeated routine; hoy can place a statement in the current day. Time expressions help interpretation but do not by themselves conjugate the verb. Keep subject agreement consistent when connecting two clauses.

An explicit pronoun can clarify contrast: yo estudio, pero mi hermano trabaja. If the subject changes, do not continue using the first verb ending automatically. Read the finished paragraph once for meaning and once for grammar, checking whether every requested detail is present. For unfamiliar vocabulary, avoid constructing a phrase only by translating each English word separately. Some useful expressions require an article or preposition that differs from English. When correcting work, preserve the original intended meaning and explain the particular ending or word-order change. More elaborate vocabulary does not compensate for an unclear subject or an omitted negative.

## Maintaining agreement when an explicit subject returns

Spanish often omits a subject pronoun when the verb ending and context make the subject clear. That omission does not remove the need for agreement. In a connected description, an explicit new subject can shift the verb form even when the topic remains the same. For regular present-tense verbs, first distinguish -ar, -er and -ir infinitives, then choose the ending for the intended subject. Negation normally places no before the conjugated verb in these simple examples. Keep time meaning separate from habitual context: the present form can describe a routine without being a progressive construction. Irregular forms and regional address conventions require separate treatment.

## Worked example 1

Complete: Nosotros ___ en Madrid. Use vivir.

1. Remove -ir to obtain viv-.

2. Choose the nosotros ending -imos for a regular -ir verb.

3. Combine them as vivimos.

Answer: Nosotros vivimos en Madrid.

## Worked example 2

Change Hablo francés into a negative statement and then make the subject mi amiga.

1. Place no before the conjugated verb: No hablo francés.

2. Mi amiga requires the third-person singular ending.

3. Use habla while retaining no before the verb.

Answer: Mi amiga no habla francés.

## Worked example 3

Combine 'I study in the morning' and 'my sister works in the afternoon' with a contrast.

1. Use estudio for the first-person singular.

2. Use trabaja for the third-person singular subject mi hermana.

3. Connect the two statements with pero and retain the different time phrases.

Answer: Estudio por la mañana, pero mi hermana trabaja por la tarde.

## Worked example 4

Translate using estudiar and vivir: “We study at home, but Elena does not live here.”

1. Nosotros or nosotras takes estudiamos for the regular -ar verb estudiar; the pronoun can be omitted here.

2. Elena requires third-person singular vive for vivir.

3. Place no before vive and retain the accent in aquí.

Answer: Estudiamos en casa, pero Elena no vive aquí.

## Misconceptions

- Usted takes the tú verb ending because both refer to you.
  Correction: Usted uses the third-person singular form, such as usted habla.

- Every Spanish verb follows the regular pattern.
  Correction: Many frequent verbs have irregular forms or stem changes and must be learned separately.
