## Both tiers: simplify, share and scale

Convert quantities to the same units before forming a ratio: 1.5 m:60 cm=150:60=5:2. Divide every term by a common factor; three-part ratios work the same way. To share 240 in ratio 2:3:5, add the parts to obtain 10, find one part as 24, then allocate 48, 72 and 120. Check the shares add to the original total. If one share rather than the total is given, find one part from that share first.

For a recipe serving 4 adapted to 10, multiply every ingredient by 10/4. A map scale 1:25,000 compares lengths in the same unit; 4 cm represents 100,000 cm=1 km. For best value compare cost per common unit, such as price per 100 g, and recognise that cheapest per unit may require buying more than needed. These are proportional models: a fixed delivery fee does not scale with the number of items.

## Both tiers: percentage change and money language

Express a part as a percentage of a whole using 100×part/whole. Percentage change uses the original value as the base: 100×(new−old)/old for old>0. The signed result distinguishes increase from decrease. A 15% increase multiplies by 1.15; a 15% discount multiplies by 0.85. These are not inverses because the second percentage acts on a different base. A deposit is a part-payment, not necessarily a discount.

Profit=selling price−cost price; loss=cost price−selling price when cost is larger. In these practice questions profit or loss percentage uses cost price unless stated otherwise; profit margin would use selling price and is a different ratio. Earnings may include a base wage and a percentage commission. A debit reduces a bank balance and a credit increases it. Tax or VAT in a question is applied to the specified taxable amount; these fictional rates illustrate arithmetic, not current financial rules.

## IGCSE both tiers: simple and compound interest

With simple interest, every period earns the same fraction of the original principal P. For rate r% each year and t years, interest I=Prt/100 and total A=P+I. With annual compound interest, each period earns interest on the current balance: A=P(1+r/100)^t. In Cambridge 0580 these formulas are not supplied for this topic, so understand and recall them. Keep the rate period and number of periods consistent.

A £500 example at 4% annually earns £20 per year if interest is simple, giving £560 after three years. Compounding gives 500×1.04³=£562.432 before rounding, or £562.43 to the nearest penny. Do not round each yearly balance unless the question specifies that rule. Interest is A−P, not A. This is a mathematical model without fees, withdrawals or changing rates; it is not a forecast.

## IGCSE Extended: reverse and repeated change

If a final price already includes a 20% increase, it is 120% of the original. Divide the final price by 1.20 to recover the original; subtracting 20% of the final price would use the wrong base. For two changes multiply the multipliers: increasing by 10% then decreasing by 10% gives 1.10×0.90=0.99, an overall 1% decrease.

For several identical percentage changes use a power of the multiplier. For different changes retain each multiplier separately. Read a repeated-change problem carefully: a loss of 5 percentage points changes a 30% rate to 25%, whereas a 5% relative decrease changes it to 28.5%. State the base and the resulting multiplier before using a calculator.

## Worked example 1

Share £315 in ratio 2:3:4. Then compare £3.60 for 600 g with £4.20 for 750 g.

1. Nine ratio parts give £35 per part; multiply by 2, 3 and 4.

2. The unit prices are £0.60 and £0.56 per 100 g, respectively.

Answer: £70, £105, £140; the 750 g pack is cheaper per 100 g.

## Worked example 2

[IGCSE] Compare simple and compound totals on £800 at 5% annually for two years.

1. Simple interest is 800×0.05×2=80; add the principal.

2. Compound total is 800×1.05²=882; the second year earns interest on £840.

Answer: Simple: £880; compound: £882.

## Worked example 3

[IGCSE Extended] A price after a 12% reduction is £66. Find the original.

1. A 12% reduction leaves 88%=0.88 of the original.

2. Solve 0.88P=66 by dividing 66 by 0.88.

Answer: £75.

## Misconceptions

- A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease cancels.
  Correction: The multipliers give 1.2×0.8=0.96: a 4% decrease.

- The compound formula gives the interest only.
  Correction: It gives the balance including principal; subtract principal to find interest.

## Flashcards

### What is one ratio part worth?
The total divided by the sum of ratio parts, if the total is given.

### What is the base for percentage change?
The original value.

### What is the simple interest formula?
I=Prt/100 for matching rate and time periods.

### What is the compound total formula?
A=P(1+r/100)^t for a fixed rate per period.

### How is an increase reversed?
Divide by its multiplier, rather than subtracting the same percentage of the final value.

## Practice 1 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Simplify 1.2 kg:800 g:2 kg and share £450 in the resulting ratio.

Answer: 3:2:5; £135, £90, £225.

1. Convert to 1200:800:2000 and divide by 400.

2. Ten parts give £45 each; multiply by 3, 2 and 5.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Simplify after matching units.

- Give all three shares summing to £450.

## Practice 2 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] A recipe for 6 needs 450 g flour and 180 mL milk. Give both quantities for 10, assuming proportional scaling.

Answer: 750 g flour and 300 mL milk.

1. Use scale factor 10/6=5/3.

2. Multiply each ingredient by the same factor.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Identify the common factor.

- Give both correctly labelled quantities.

## Practice 3 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] On a 1:40,000 map a route measures 7.5 cm. Find the actual distance in km. Separately, which is cheaper per kg: £2.70 for 600 g or £3.40 for 800 g?

Answer: 3 km; the 800 g pack (£4.25/kg versus £4.50/kg).

1. 7.5×40000=300000 cm=3 km.

2. Divide each price by mass in kilograms.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Convert the map distance to km.

- Compare both unit prices and choose the 800 g pack.

## Practice 4 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] An item costs £48 and sells for £60. Calculate profit and profit as a percentage of cost. What would the loss percentage be if it sold for £42 instead?

Answer: £12 profit, 25% profit; 12.5% loss in the second case.

1. Profit percentage is 12/48×100.

2. The alternative loss is £6, and 6/48×100=12.5.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Give profit and its cost-based percentage.

- Give the correct loss percentage.

## Practice 5 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] A £240 item has a 15% discount. A customer pays a 25% deposit on the discounted price. Find the price, deposit and outstanding balance.

Answer: £204, £51 and £153.

1. Apply 240×0.85=204.

2. The deposit is 204/4=51; subtract it to find 153.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Apply the discount to the original price.

- Apply the deposit to the discounted price and give the balance.

## Practice 6 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] A fictional weekly pay scheme gives £320 plus 6% of £1500 sales. Find total pay and express it as a percentage of the £320 base.

Answer: £410; 128.125% of the base.

1. Commission is 0.06×1500=90.

2. Total is 320+90=410; 100×410/320=128.125.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Calculate total earnings.

- Use the base as denominator for a percentage greater than 100.

## Practice 7 — standard · 2 marks

[IGCSE Core/Extended] Compare total and interest on £1200 at 3% annually for three years, simple versus compound. Round final money only to pennies.

Answer: Simple: total £1308, interest £108. Compound: total £1311.27, interest £111.27.

1. Simple interest=1200×0.03×3=108.

2. Compound total=1200×1.03³=1311.2724; subtract 1200 before final rounding.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Give simple total and interest.

- Use compound power and give both rounded results.

## Practice 8 — extension · 2 marks

[IGCSE Extended] A bill including a fictional 15% tax is £92. Find the pre-tax amount and the tax charged.

Answer: £80 before tax; £12 tax.

1. The total is 1.15 times the pre-tax amount.

2. 92/1.15=80 and 92−80=12.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Divide by 1.15 rather than subtracting 15% of £92.

- Give both amounts.

## Practice 9 — extension · 2 marks

[IGCSE Extended] A value rises by 25% and then falls by 20%. Find the overall percentage change. Would reversing the order change the multiplier?

Answer: 0% change; no, 1.25×0.80=1 in either order.

1. Multiply the two multipliers rather than adding the rates.

2. Multiplication commutes for the same pair of proportional changes.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Compute the product as 1.

- Explain the zero change and order independence for this model.

## Practice 10 — standard · 2 marks

[GCSE Foundation/Higher; IGCSE Core/Extended] Red beads are 5/12 of a bag containing only red and blue beads. Give red:blue and find the number of red beads in a bag of 84.

Answer: 5:7; 35 red beads.

1. The blue fraction is 7/12, so compare the two part counts.

2. 84×5/12=35.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Convert the part-to-whole fraction into 5:7.

- Apply the fraction as an operator.

## Revision guide

Ratios need common units, percentages need a named base, and interest needs a stated time period. Reverse problems divide by a multiplier; repeated changes multiply multipliers. These models use invented prices and rates.

### Key ideas

- What is one ratio part worth? The total divided by the sum of ratio parts, if the total is given.

- What is the base for percentage change? The original value.

- What is the simple interest formula? I=Prt/100 for matching rate and time periods.

- What is the compound total formula? A=P(1+r/100)^t for a fixed rate per period.

- How is an increase reversed? Divide by its multiplier, rather than subtracting the same percentage of the final value.
