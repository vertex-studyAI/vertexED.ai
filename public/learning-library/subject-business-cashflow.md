## A cash forecast follows timing

A cash-flow forecast records expected receipts and payments in the periods when cash is expected to move. Opening balance plus receipts minus payments gives closing balance. The next period begins with the previous closing balance unless a separate adjustment is specified. A sale on credit can increase revenue before cash is received, and buying equipment can create a large cash payment even though its cost is recognised over time under an accounting policy. Identify whether each number is an amount owed, earned or actually paid before placing it in a cash schedule.

## Profit is not cash availability

Profit compares recognised revenue and expenses over an accounting period; cash flow tracks movements of cash. A profitable business can face a cash shortage if customers pay late while suppliers and employees must be paid earlier. A loan can improve cash availability without becoming sales revenue or profit. Conversely, repaying loan principal reduces cash but is not itself an operating expense. Keep interest separate from principal. These distinctions prevent a positive bank balance from being treated as proof that operations are profitable or a low balance from proving the opposite.

## Evaluate responses and forecast uncertainty

A projected shortage can be addressed through payment timing, borrowing, owner finance or operating changes, but each response has costs and constraints. Delaying suppliers may damage relationships; offering an early-payment discount reduces receipts; borrowing adds repayment obligations and possibly interest. Test the forecast under plausible changes in sales and payment dates rather than treating estimates as facts. Working capital commonly means current assets minus current liabilities, not cash alone. This lesson uses invented business cases for study and does not provide a financing recommendation for an actual business.

## Worked example 1

An invented shop starts a month with 400 units of cash, receives 900 and pays 1,100. Find its closing balance.

1. Use opening+receipts-payments.

2. Calculate 400+900-1,100.

Answer: 200 units.

## Worked example 2

A firm makes a 600-unit credit sale and receives a 1,000-unit loan today. No customer cash arrives today. Identify today's cash receipt and sales revenue.

1. The loan brings in 1,000 cash but is a liability, not sales revenue.

2. The completed credit sale creates 600 revenue under the stated recognition assumption, but no cash receipt yet.

Answer: Cash receipts 1,000; sales revenue 600.

## Misconceptions

- Positive profit guarantees enough cash to pay every bill.
  Correction: Recognition and payment timing can differ.

- Borrowing improves operating profit by the loan amount.
  Correction: It increases cash and liabilities, not sales revenue.

## Flashcards

### How is closing cash balance calculated?
Opening balance plus receipts minus payments.

### Does a credit sale necessarily bring cash immediately?
No. Payment may arrive in a later period.

### Is a loan receipt sales revenue?
No. It creates a repayment obligation.

### What is working capital in the common accounting definition?
Current assets minus current liabilities.

### Why run a sensitivity check on a forecast?
Receipts, costs and payment dates are uncertain estimates.

## Practice 1 — standard · 2 marks

An invented firm opens with 250, receives 780 and pays 910. Find closing cash.

Answer: 120 units.

1. Add opening balance to receipts.

2. Subtract payments: 250+780-910.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Find 120.

## Practice 2 — standard · 2 marks

Month one closes with 120. Month two receipts are 500 and payments 760, with no new finance. Find month two's closing balance.

Answer: -140 units, indicating a projected financing shortfall.

1. Carry 120 forward as the opening balance.

2. Calculate 120+500-760.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Carry the balance correctly.

- Find and interpret -140.

## Practice 3 — standard · 2 marks

Current assets are 4,200 and current liabilities are 3,100. Calculate working capital and explain whether it is necessarily all cash.

Answer: 1,100 units; it may include inventory and receivables as well as cash.

1. Subtract current liabilities from current assets.

2. Distinguish the asset categories within the total.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Find 1,100.

- Explain why it is not cash alone.

## Practice 4 — extension · 2 marks

An otherwise unchanged forecast expected 300 from a customer this month, but payment moves to next month. Explain this month's closing-cash effect and whether sales revenue must change.

Answer: This month's closing cash is 300 lower. Revenue need not change solely because the collection date changes.

1. Move the receipt to the later period.

2. Separate receipt timing from the stated revenue-recognition basis.

Marking points (one practice mark each):

- Identify the 300 cash reduction.

- Separate cash timing from revenue.
