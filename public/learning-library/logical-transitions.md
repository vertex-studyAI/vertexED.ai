## Objectives

- Identify a relationship before choosing a transition.

- Preserve the direction and scope of a connection.

- Check punctuation separately from logical meaning.

## Read the relationship before the options

A transition tells the reader how one idea connects to another. Cover the choices initially and summarise each sentence in a few words. Then name the relationship in ordinary language: the second gives an example, contradicts an expectation, adds a parallel point or follows as a consequence. Selecting a word because it sounds formal can hide a mismatch between the actual claims.

Read enough surrounding context to identify the relevant pair of ideas. The connection may involve an entire preceding explanation rather than only its final noun. A repeated topic word shows subject continuity, but it does not establish whether the relationship is agreement, contrast or cause. Two sentences about the same experiment may still make opposing claims about its reliability.

## Addition and illustration

An additive transition introduces another compatible point. An illustrative transition introduces a specific instance of a broader claim. These relationships are easy to confuse because both can extend a discussion. Ask whether the second statement is another independent reason or an example belonging to the category named in the first. If the first says several materials can be reused and the second names reused timber, illustration is likely.

Do not use an example marker when the second sentence merely restates the same general idea. Nor should a specific example be treated as proof of every possible case. A transition can organise evidence but cannot strengthen the evidence beyond what it establishes. Some sentences need no explicit marker because the relationship is already clear through syntax and reference.

## Contrast and concession

A contrast highlights a difference between claims or subjects. A concession acknowledges a point and then presents something that holds despite the expectation it might create. For example, a design may be inexpensive yet difficult to maintain. The low initial cost might suggest an advantage, but the maintenance difficulty qualifies that advantage. Words such as however and nevertheless can signal different shades of opposition depending on context.

The presence of a negative word does not automatically require a contrast marker. Two negative observations may support the same conclusion. Conversely, two positive descriptions can contrast on a shared criterion, such as one material being flexible and another rigid. Identify the conceptual relationship instead of matching isolated keywords. A credible transition must connect the propositions as written, not a causal story imagined by the reader.

## Cause, result and chronology

A result transition presents the second idea as following from the first. Check that the first supplies a reason sufficient for the stated inference in context. Chronology alone is weaker: one event can occur after another without being caused by it. A phrase meaning subsequently should not be replaced with therefore unless the passage supports that stronger causal or logical relation.

Direction matters. A consequence follows its stated cause, whereas an explanation supplies a reason for an earlier claim. Reversing the order of two sentences can require a different connection even when their facts stay unchanged. In an argument, a conclusion marker may indicate an inference from evidence rather than a physical causal process. In both cases, the inference must be supported instead of merely asserted by the connector.

## Punctuation and reference are separate checks

A semicolon can connect two closely related independent clauses, and a conjunctive adverb can follow it when the logic fits. For example, The first trial failed; nevertheless, the team repeated it with improved controls uses punctuation to join complete clauses and a transition to express persistence despite failure. The punctuation does not determine whether nevertheless is logically appropriate; that depends on the ideas.

Do not assume a transition fixes an incomplete sentence or a comma splice. Conversely, an option may be grammatically well punctuated yet express the wrong relationship. For this lesson, explain logic first and then inspect clause boundaries, commas and referents. If the task supplies surrounding punctuation, evaluate the candidate within that exact structure rather than silently rewriting other words.

## Checking alternatives and scope

When two choices seem plausible, replace each transition with a plain-language statement of its meaning. Compare that paraphrase with the passage. For instance, as a result commits to consequence, while in addition merely adds information. Ask which claim the writer actually has evidence to make. An option can be rejected for being too strong even if the events could be related in some unstated situation.

## Choosing a contrast without inventing a cause

A transition specifies how the next statement relates to the previous one. Before choosing a word, summarise that relationship without using any option. A contrast can qualify an encouraging result without claiming the result caused a limitation. Conversely, a result transition such as therefore requires a justified inferential connection, not merely two adjacent statements. After choosing the relationship, check that punctuation suits the clause structure. A semicolon can join two independent clauses before however; a comma alone usually creates a splice in this pattern. The original examples below separate the logical decision from the punctuation decision.

## Worked example 1

Original passage: Many surfaces absorb sound. Thick fabric panels, ___, can reduce reflected sound in a room. Choose a relationship.

1. The first sentence makes a broad claim.

2. Fabric panels are a particular member of that category.

3. An illustration marker fits.

Answer: For example.

## Worked example 2

Original passage: The route was longer than expected. ___, the group arrived before noon. Explain a suitable transition.

1. A longer route might create an expectation of later arrival.

2. The second sentence reports success despite that expectation.

3. Use a concessive relation rather than a claimed cause.

Answer: Nevertheless is suitable.

## Worked example 3

Original passage: The switch disconnected the circuit. ___, current through that branch stopped. Identify the relation.

1. The first event interrupts the conducting path.

2. The second is a consequence within the stated simple circuit context.

3. Choose a result marker.

Answer: Consequently.

## Worked example 4

Original passage: The committee met on Monday. It announced a decision on Wednesday. Is therefore automatically justified?

1. The notes establish a time sequence.

2. They do not by themselves explain the reasoning behind the decision.

3. A chronological connection is safer than an unsupported inference.

Answer: No; chronology alone does not justify therefore.

## Worked example 5

Choose and justify a transition: “The new route reduced the journey by ten minutes. ___, it required passengers to change buses twice.” Options: However; Therefore; For example.

1. The first statement reports a benefit.

2. The second introduces a contrasting inconvenience rather than an example of time saved.

3. Therefore would claim a result relationship that the passage does not establish.

Answer: However. The second sentence presents a drawback that qualifies the time saving.

## Misconceptions

- Any two sentences on the same topic need an additive transition.
  Correction: They may contrast, exemplify or stand in a causal relation; inspect their claims.

- Therefore simply means later.
  Correction: It signals an inference or consequence, which requires more support than chronology.

- Correct punctuation guarantees the correct transition.
  Correction: Grammar and logical relationship are separate checks.

## Flashcards

### What should you do before looking at transition choices?
Summarise the connected ideas and identify their relationship.

### What does an additive transition signal?
Another compatible point, not necessarily an example or consequence.

### What relation does for example introduce?
A specific instance of a broader claim.

### What must a contrast marker connect?
A genuine difference or opposition between the relevant ideas.

### What distinguishes concession?
Something holds despite a point that might create a contrary expectation.

### What extra commitment does consequently make?
That the second idea follows as a result or supported consequence.

### Why is afterward not equivalent to therefore?
It marks time order without necessarily asserting a causal or logical result.

### Why does sentence order matter for a transition?
It changes which idea is presented as reason, result or qualification.

### Can a transition alone repair a comma splice?
No; clause-boundary punctuation must also be correct.

### How can you test two plausible transitions?
Paraphrase each relationship and compare its strength and direction with the evidence.

### What must therefore be supported by?
A defensible result or inference relationship between the statements.

### Why can however require a semicolon before it?
When it connects two independent clauses in one sentence, it does not itself license a comma splice.
