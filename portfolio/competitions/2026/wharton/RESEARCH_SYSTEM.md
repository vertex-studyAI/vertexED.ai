# Wharton Investment Competition — Research Operating System

Status: prep system, 2026-08-28

The objective is not to maximize short-run simulated return. Build a coherent client-specific investment process whose decisions, risks, and revisions can be defended.

## Team operating roles

Assign owners, not silos. Everyone should understand the final portfolio.

- CIO / strategy: client interpretation, portfolio thesis, final consistency
- macro: rates, inflation, growth, scenarios, cross-asset implications
- equity analyst A: sector/company research
- equity analyst B: valuation/catalysts
- portfolio/risk: sizing, concentration, scenario downside, portfolio math
- editor/presenter: evidence consistency, report narrative, visual communication

## Client memo

Before discussing securities, write:

### Objectives
- return objective
- income/liquidity needs
- time horizon
- real-world purpose of the portfolio

### Constraints
- risk tolerance
- liquidity
- concentration
- sector/geographic restrictions
- ethical/mission constraints if stated in the case

### Success definition
One paragraph stating what a good portfolio outcome means for this exact client.

## Investment Policy Statement skeleton

1. client summary
2. objectives
3. constraints
4. strategic asset allocation logic
5. diversification principles
6. risk controls
7. security-selection process
8. monitoring/rebalancing rules
9. conditions that trigger thesis review

## Company memo template

### One-line thesis
Why this security belongs in this client's portfolio now.

### Business
- revenue drivers
- economics/margins
- competitive position
- key industry structure

### Variant view
What does the market appear to under/overestimate?

### Evidence
Use primary filings/data where possible. Separate observed facts from team interpretation.

### Valuation
At minimum:
- current valuation metric(s)
- historical/peer context
- base/bull/bear assumptions
- what price or fundamental change makes the expected return unattractive

### Catalysts
Specific events/changes with an approximate time horizon.

### Risks
At least three serious thesis-breaking risks. Do not list generic boilerplate.

### Client fit
Why this holding serves the client's objective better than cash, an index, or a plausible alternative.

### Falsification conditions
Write observable conditions that would force the team to revise or exit.

## Macro memo template

For each weekly update:
- what changed
- evidence/source
- market implication
- portfolio implication
- confidence (low/medium/high)
- what would change the view

Avoid predictions that cannot be tied to a portfolio decision.

## Decision journal

For every buy/sell/resize decision record:
- date
- decision
- evidence available at the time
- expected portfolio role
- strongest dissenting argument
- invalidation condition
- size before/after
- follow-up date

Do not rewrite the original rationale after outcomes are known.

## Portfolio risk dashboard

Track at minimum:
- position weights
- sector exposure
- geographic exposure
- concentration top 3/top 5
- cash
- portfolio beta or another declared market-risk proxy if available
- scenario P/L estimates with assumptions
- thesis status: intact / watch / broken

Do not create fake precision from unreliable covariance estimates on a small school competition portfolio.

## Weekly investment committee

Each holding gets 3 minutes:
1. Why do we own it?
2. What changed this week?
3. What could prove us wrong?
4. Is current sizing still justified?

Then one challenger must argue the opposite side.

End the meeting with a written action list and owners.

## Quality score for every proposed holding

Score 0–2 each:
- client fit
- evidence quality
- valuation support
- catalyst clarity
- downside understanding
- diversification contribution
- falsifiability

A holding scoring below 10/14 needs more research before inclusion.

## Pre-case drills

Before the official case work becomes intense, complete:
- 2 sample client memos
- 5 company memos across different sectors
- 2 macro memos
- 1 mock IPS
- 1 scenario-analysis exercise
- 2 simulated investment-committee meetings

The goal is to make the research process automatic before the final report matters.

## Evidence rules

- source every material number
- distinguish reporting period from publication date
- use the information set allowed by the competition rules
- no hindsight edits to the decision journal
- no unsupported target prices
- state assumptions in valuation/scenarios
- if a thesis changes, record why

## Five questions every final holding must survive

1. Why this asset?
2. Why now?
3. Why for this client?
4. What evidence would prove the thesis wrong?
5. What happens to the portfolio if we are wrong?
