## 2. Frozen venture thesis

**VertexED is an exam-preparation operating loop for secondary-school students: plan the week, focus, practise under board-shaped constraints, review against assessment criteria, and schedule retrieval of weak concepts.**

The competition thesis should not be “AI study app.” That category is crowded. The sharper problem is that students use separate planning, content, practice, grading, and flashcard tools, so evidence from one study step rarely changes the next step. VertexED’s defensible wedge is the connection between **exam-format practice and mark-loss feedback** inside the same revision loop.

## 5. Problem evidence

The narrative can safely argue the structural problem without inventing survey statistics:

1. Students commonly separate planning, past-paper practice, flashcards, explanation, and feedback across different tools.
2. A disconnected workflow creates a coordination problem: weak performance in a mock does not automatically become the next retrieval task or schedule change.
3. For IB MYP and smaller/niche programs, high-quality paper-shaped practice and worked feedback can be harder to find than generic study content.
4. Generative AI can answer a question without teaching the student how the mark scheme or command term actually rewards reasoning.

For the final submission, add at least **10 short user interviews or structured beta interviews** focused on behavior, not praise. Record:

- what students used in the week before a mock;
- how many tools/tabs they switched among;
- how they chose what to revise next;
- how they turned a marked paper into the next study action;
- which board/subject lacked realistic practice;
- what they currently pay for, if anything.

The goal is not a vanity satisfaction score. The goal is evidence that the workflow break exists.

### VertexED differentiation to defend

Do not claim that competitors lack planning, mocks, or AI. Instead claim a narrower product architecture:

**VertexED is designed so a board-shaped practice result changes the next planning and retrieval action.**

A credible final demonstration should show one complete loop:

1. set an exam date and a weak topic;
2. generate or select a paper-shaped task;
3. complete it under time;
4. receive mark-scheme-aware feedback naming lost marks;
5. convert that weakness into a scheduled follow-up retrieval task.

If the product cannot demonstrate this end-to-end, the competition thesis should be weakened rather than papered over.

- Free: one usable revision loop with constrained AI/generation volume.
- Student Pro: higher paper-generation/feedback limits, deeper analytics, and longer history.

## 9. Competition positioning

Track: **Business Innovation**.

Primary customer: secondary-school students preparing for board-style assessments, initially those in IB MYP/DP and other programs where realistic practice and assessment-criteria feedback are fragmented.

Beachhead use case: the 7–14 days before a mock/exam, when students already have urgency and need a concrete revision sequence.

High-level value proposition:

> VertexED turns a marked practice attempt into the next study action, so students spend less time stitching together tools and more time closing the exact gaps that cost marks.
