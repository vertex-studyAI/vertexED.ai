## Immediate Top 5 research briefs

### 1. `T2424-0025` — Non-Gaussian Memory Transformer

**Research question.** Under heavy-tailed corruption in attention-addressed memory, can robust aggregation improve retrieval without paying an unacceptable clean-data cost?

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** The currently observed synthetic advantage of the weighted median is specific enough to survive on a frozen learned sequence-retrieval task and across multiple heavy-tail contamination families, while clean-control performance stays within a predeclared tolerance.

**Baseline.** Same attention weights with arithmetic-mean aggregation.

**Dataset/environment.** Next gate should be a small generated sequence-retrieval dataset with train/validation/test splits, frozen before test evaluation. Add contamination only after the clean retrieval task is working.

**Primary metric.** Retrieval accuracy/error under contamination, with clean-control accuracy and calibration as required guardrails.

**Ablations.** Mean vs weighted median vs Huber vs trimmed mean; contamination family; contamination fraction; sequence length; key-noise level; same attention weights across aggregators.

**Smallest meaningful experiment.** Train a small reference retrieval model once, freeze weights, then evaluate only the memory readout across clean/Gaussian/Cauchy/Student-t corruption using >=10 fixed seeds.

**Compute.** CPU or small local GPU; deliberately sub-hour/small-model scale, not an 8–12B run.

**Expected failures.** Median can discard useful graded values; gains may disappear once memory values are learned; robust readout may hurt smooth Gaussian cases; contamination may be unrealistically separable.

**Paper-worthy threshold.** Robust aggregation must show a reproducible interaction: materially larger benefit under heavy tails than clean/Gaussian controls, across more than one task/contamination family, with confidence intervals and no hidden threshold tuning.

**Next implementation step.** Add the frozen learned-retrieval benchmark and multi-aggregator evaluation while leaving the current synthetic reference untouched.

### 2. `T2424-0029` — Representation Phase Transitions for PDEs

**Research question.** Do measured representation-dimension changes track meaningful changes in PDE dynamics, and do learned representations show the same transition structure as classical spectral bases at matched reconstruction error?

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** At least one nonlinear PDE exhibits reproducible effective-dimension transitions that are robust to grid resolution and threshold choice, and a learned latent representation changes dimension in a correlated but nontrivial way relative to Fourier/POD baselines.

**Baseline.** Fourier effective dimension; add POD/SVD as a second classical baseline.

**Dataset/environment.** Small numerical nonlinear PDE benchmark (for example Burgers-like periodic dynamics) with frozen parameter sweep and train/validation/test trajectories.

**Primary metric.** Effective dimension at matched reconstruction error, plus transition location stability across resolution/threshold sweeps.

**Ablations.** Energy/reconstruction threshold; grid size; time horizon; viscosity/forcing parameter; Fourier vs POD vs learned autoencoder latent; seed/model width.

**Smallest meaningful experiment.** One nonlinear PDE, three resolutions, three predeclared reconstruction thresholds, Fourier/POD plus one tiny autoencoder.

**Compute.** Laptop/small GPU feasible; trajectories can be generated numerically at modest resolution.

**Expected failures.** “Transitions” may be threshold artifacts; learned dimension may simply track reconstruction capacity; nonlinear dynamics may smear discrete changes into continuous trends.

**Paper-worthy threshold.** Transition locations/effective-dimension changes remain stable under reasonable resolution/threshold variation and predict a measurable change in forecast/reconstruction difficulty better than simple energy/entropy summaries.

**Next implementation step.** Extend the frozen analytic package with one nonlinear solver dataset and threshold/resolution sensitivity report; do not rename operational rank changes as universal phase transitions.

### 3. `T2424-0026` — Counterfactual Defect Worlds

**Research question.** Can causal-intervention diagnostics detect whether a learned/local world model propagates localized defects within the physically or architecturally permitted influence region?

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** With paired initial conditions and shared randomness, a model trained on local dynamics will keep counterfactual divergence substantially more localized than a nonlocal/misspecified control, and violations predict downstream rollout error.

**Baseline.** Exact simulator paired-world divergence; nonlocal or shuffled-dynamics control.

**Dataset/environment.** First extend to stochastic cellular dynamics with shared seeds; then one small physical simulator or learned local neural cellular/world-model benchmark.

**Primary metric.** Causal-cone violation rate plus divergence-vs-time; secondary metric rollout error correlation.

**Ablations.** Intervention type/location/time; local vs nonlocal model; shared vs unshared randomness control; defect magnitude; Hamming vs state-space/semantic distance.

**Smallest meaningful experiment.** Train/fit one tiny local learned dynamics model on a 1D/2D cellular system, inject held-out interventions, and compare causal localization to the exact simulator.

**Compute.** CPU/small GPU; no large generative world model required.

**Expected failures.** Learned models can blur local influence even when predictions are good; metric may be trivial under deterministic systems; semantic defects may not map to Hamming divergence.

**Paper-worthy threshold.** Intervention diagnostics reveal a reproducible failure mode not captured by ordinary rollout MSE/accuracy and transfer to at least two dynamics families or one physical benchmark.

**Next implementation step.** Add stochastic paired-world support with shared RNG and a tiny learned local dynamics baseline.

### 4. `T2424-0027` — Sapir–Whorf Latent Tongue

**Research question.** In real multilingual embeddings, can language-predictive structure be reduced while preserving task/concept information, and what representation geometry is removed in the process?

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** A pre-registered language-centroid/subspace removal reduces held-out language predictability beyond a global-centering control while preserving a predeclared semantic/task metric within tolerance on at least one public multilingual model/dataset.

**Baseline.** Raw embeddings and global centering; optionally a stronger linear language-subspace removal baseline, predeclared before final test labels.

**Dataset/environment.** Public multilingual model + public aligned multilingual dataset; fixed layer(s), languages and split frozen in advance.

**Primary metric.** Excess language-predictability reduction relative to chance, with semantic/task retention as a hard guardrail.

**Ablations.** Layer; language set; transform family; centroid-fit data size; aligned vs unaligned data; concept/task probe type.

**Smallest meaningful experiment.** One open multilingual encoder, three languages, one aligned classification/retrieval dataset, one frozen layer, train/calibration/test separation.

**Compute.** Embedding extraction on a small public model is laptop/GPU feasible; no model training required for the first real-data test.

**Expected failures.** Language and semantics may be entangled; centroid removal may exploit parallel-data structure; probe choice can create false “leakage” wins; performance may be layer-specific.

**Paper-worthy threshold.** Replicated reduction across multiple layers/models or datasets with retained semantic performance and stronger controls, without making linguistic-relativity claims unsupported by the experiment.

**Next implementation step.** Pre-register the public model/dataset/layer/split protocol as a new scientific line; do not inherit the synthetic PASS verdict.

### 5. `T2424-0024` — Trust Under Uncertainty

**Research question.** Which calibration/selective-prediction metrics actually separate useful confidence policies on held-out model predictions, and when do ranking-only risk/coverage measures conceal miscalibration?

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** On held-out real predictions, calibration-aware metrics distinguish confidence policies that have similar accuracy/ranking, and calibrated probabilities improve Brier/ECE without degrading selective risk beyond a predeclared tolerance.

**Baseline.** Raw model confidence; compare temperature scaling or another simple calibration method fit only on calibration data.

**Dataset/environment.** Frozen predictions from at least one small public classifier/LLM task with separate calibration and test splits; include difficulty/domain slices.

**Primary metric.** Brier score as primary; ECE and selective risk/coverage as complementary metrics with bootstrap confidence intervals.

**Ablations.** Calibration method; binning strategy; coverage threshold; model/task; difficulty/domain slice; confidence source.

**Smallest meaningful experiment.** One public classifier and dataset, frozen logits, calibration split, untouched test split, bootstrap intervals and raw-vs-calibrated comparison.

**Compute.** Very low; predictions can be cached and evaluator is dependency-light.

**Expected failures.** ECE instability under binning; calibration may improve Brier while harming decision utility; selective-risk ranking may remain identical; dataset shift may dominate calibration method.

**Paper-worthy threshold.** A reproducible practical finding across multiple model/task pairs showing where calibration metrics disagree and which metric/abstention policy predicts real selective-decision utility.

**Next implementation step.** Freeze one real held-out prediction file and calibration protocol, then run the existing independent metric recomputation unchanged.
